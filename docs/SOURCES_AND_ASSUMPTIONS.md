Sources and Assumptions
1. Purpose

This document identifies which scientific concepts in the single-player Earth-observation MVP are supported by NASA references and which values are simplified assumptions created for gameplay.

The goal is to make the distinction between real scientific information and game-design decisions clear.

Important: Illustrative values are not official NASA specifications or certified engineering data.

2. Classification
NASA-supported: Scientific concepts or facts supported by an official NASA reference.
Illustrative assumption: Simplified values or rules created for gameplay and balancing.
Pending verification: Values that require checking against the project's actual catalog or an appropriate source.

4.3 Solar panels

NASA-supported concept:

Solar panels convert sunlight into electrical energy. Batteries can store energy for periods when a spacecraft cannot receive direct sunlight.

Illustrative assumptions:

Any panel power ratings, prices, masses or gameplay efficiency multipliers used by the game must be treated as illustrative unless a specific supporting source is documented.

Scientific reference: NASA – What Powers a Spacecraft?

https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/what-powers-a-spacecraft/en/

4.4 Communications

NASA-supported concept:

Satellites use antennas and communication systems to send and receive information between spacecraft and Earth.

Illustrative assumptions:

Communication scores, contact multipliers, data transfer bonuses and simplified ground-station availability are gameplay assumptions unless independently verified.

4.5 Science instruments

NASA-supported concept:

Earth-observation satellites carry scientific instruments that collect information about Earth's land, atmosphere, oceans and other environmental features.

Illustrative assumptions:

Instrument prices, science scores, observation bonuses and simplified effectiveness multipliers are gameplay assumptions unless supported by a specific reference.

4.6 Launch systems

Pending verification:

Launch vehicle specifications, payload limits, launch prices and other launch-catalog values must be checked against the actual project catalog and appropriate sources before being classified as NASA-supported or illustrative.

5. Important Scientific Definitions
Wh (watt-hour): A unit of energy used to describe battery storage capacity.
W (watt): A unit of power.
Orbital period: The time required for a satellite to complete one revolution around Earth.
Altitude: The height of a satellite above Earth's surface.
Sunlight fraction: The fraction of modeled time that a spacecraft spends in sunlight, represented by a number between 0 and 1.
Assumption: A simplified choice used by the game instead of modeling all real-world physical processes.
6. Rules for Scientific Accuracy
Never describe an invented gameplay value as NASA-certified.
Clearly label simplified gameplay modifiers.
Use official NASA resources when explaining scientific concepts.
Record the source and publication date when relevant.
Do not use a scientific reference to justify unrelated invented prices or game balances.
Keep scientific explanations separate from gameplay balancing decisions.
7. Limitations

This document records the battery and orbit examples currently discussed for the MVP.

The remaining mission, instrument, solar, communications and launch catalogs must be inspected and their actual values added before this document can be considered a complete inventory of all game components.

8. Conclusion

The game uses real scientific concepts as its foundation while simplifying selected values and mechanics to make the experience accessible and enjoyable.

NASA references support the educational explanations. Invented values are explicitly identified as gameplay assumptions so players and reviewers can distinguish scientific information from game balancing.
